# Final Remarks from the Authors

*(Post at the end of the discussion period, after individual replies have been addressed.)*

We sincerely thank all reviewers for their thoughtful and constructive feedback. The reviews were specific enough to point directly at the experiments worth running, and the paper is substantially stronger as a result — including in the places where those experiments went against us.

## Common Strengths Highlighted by Reviewers

* **The self-contained vs. referential framing.** Reviewers noted it "cleanly explains why AO is near-perfect in some settings and collapses to zero in others" (**iNYK**), is "well motivated" (**Vg97**), and is "a meaningful conceptual improvement over treating all assistant-generated context as uniformly harmful" (**5YHP**).
* **The structural-exclusion (contagious pollution) result.** All three reviewers highlighted this as a central contribution, with **5YHP** calling it "one of the strongest parts of the paper" and noting it suggests "information-flow constraints, rather than prompt instructions alone, may be necessary," and **iNYK** noting it "carries a useful lesson for any multi-stage LLM pipeline."
* **Modularity and interpretability** of the specification-extraction, approach-evaluation, and intervention separation (**5YHP**).

## Summary of Key Revisions

* **A unified method statement, empirically demonstrated at two levels.** Addressing the Area Chair, **Vg97 (W3, Q4)**, and **5YHP (W2)**, we add a component table separating the essential analyzer and structural-exclusion condition from the single tunable operator knob; we evaluate **the same four operators across 3 respondent models x 4 LiC tasks, plus CollabLLM, WildChat, and tau2**, with no per-benchmark tuning; and we swap the **analyzer** across five models from four families with the respondent held fixed, where every analyzer is positive and individually significant (**+12.9 to +39.9pp**, n=178 matched pairs) and weak analyzers under-detect rather than mis-detect.

* **Scaled evaluation and paired significance testing.** Addressing **iNYK (W1, Q2)**, **Vg97 (W2, Q2)**, and **5YHP (W3)**, LiC now uses **up to 50 problems per task across 3 conversation prefixes** (up to 150 conversations per cell) on **3 models**, up from 18-25 on 1 model. We replace best-of-3 with mean +/- std and add paired tests on raw accuracy: **AC3-Reset improves over full context on 33 of 36 paired comparisons (+15.9pp, sign-test p < 0.0001)**, above the assistant-omission design-oracle's +13.3pp. Because Vg97 asked for intervals rather than tests alone, we also report the item-level version on 1,668 paired problems: **+15.4pp, 95% CI [+11.5, +19.4]** by a problem-clustered bootstrap, 350 problem-wins to 93. We also state plainly where AC3 does and does not separate from that oracle: head to head the matrix-wide margin is +2.6pp on 15 wins / 17 losses / 4 ties — **+2.8pp with a 95% CI of [−0.3, +5.9]** at the item level, which includes zero and which we therefore do not claim — concentrated on LiC-database (+18.7pp, 8 of 9 triples; interval [+10.7, +26.6]) and on the settings where omission is structurally unusable, which is the operator-by-regime claim the paper makes.

* **A condensation baseline at measured matched compute.** Addressing **Vg97 (W1, Q1)** and the Area Chair, we implement and run a summarisation baseline and an MT-OSC reimplementation on our two highest-pollution tasks. This experiment runs on the **complete, unselected** LiC pool (n=107 database / 100 code) end-to-end rather than on a difficulty-selected replay subset, which is why its full-context baseline (56.1 / 83.0) sits far above Table 1's — the measured single-turn ceiling on that pool is 94.4 / 98.0, so it still carries a 38.3 / 15.0pp multi-turn gap. Summarisation moves accuracy **down** (−2.8 / −8.4pp database, −4.0 / −3.0pp code) while **AC3-Reset gains +19.6 / +9.0pp**, closing 51% and 60% of that gap against 50% on Table 1's harder subset; the budget-matched summariser **over-consumed** AC3-Reset (1.02–1.19x strategy calls, 1.62–2.14x strategy tokens) and still lost by 12 to 28 points. MT-OSC at its published window engages 0.3 times per conversation on 4.1-turn conversations; scaling its window to the conversation length (w=2) makes it engage **nine times more often and score worse** (47.7%, −13.1pp against its own w=4 run, p = 0.016; AC3-Reset +28.0pp ahead), which we report as a scoping result about length-triggered compaction rather than as a beaten baseline. Because we wrote the condenser's prompt, we also ran a **neutral-prompt variant** with the "compression, not evaluation" clause deleted: it scores **51.4%**, between two replicate runs of our own prompt, and it flags an assistant error **0 times in 340 summaries** — exactly as often as with the clause — so the result does not depend on our phrasing. In the same pass we place the **ERGO** baseline on the same filtered conversation pools as every other row, which raises it — see correction 7 below.

* **A direct, judge-free evaluation of the analyzer as a detector, and a causal ablation of naturally occurring content.** Addressing **5YHP (W5)**, we first inject one known-false and one known-true span per conversation and measure removal, preservation, edit precision and gate sensitivity against ground truth, with four offline positive controls: the analyzer **names** the injected pollutant in 78.6% of conversations. We then close that study's own stated limitation — that injected pollution may be more salient than natural pollution — with a counterfactual span ablation on **111 naturally occurring spans** across 30 conversations, each re-run 14 times present and 12 times removed, with no detector and no judge anywhere in the label path. Natural pollution is **real and concentrated** (per-span effect spread 0.155 against a replicate-matched null's 0.125, p = 0.0085; 16 large-effect spans where the null predicts 9.3, p = 0.017) while the typical span is causally inert, and **AC3 removes 100% of the spans the ablation proves harmful**. The same study shows that **neither** operator is selective, which retracts a claim in the paper (below).

* **A mechanism test for auditing vs. re-solving.** Addressing **5YHP (W1)**, we measure how often the analyzer's own output contains the verified correct answer and re-run the paired comparison on the leak-free subset: **+30.2pp on code with 0% leakage**, **+26.0pp on database**, pooling to **+20.7pp [+14.8, +25.3], p < 0.0001**. On **math** the leak-free gain is **−2.6pp** and we concede it explicitly.

* **A memory split and variance analysis.** Addressing **5YHP (W6)**, contamination is measurably zero (0 of 120 duplicates against the canonical evaluation set; 0.0pp effect in a within-instance transductive probe), while the cheatsheet learner itself is high-variance (~6pp), which is a limitation we state ourselves rather than defending single-trial gains.

* **An unbiased, end-to-end experiment.** Addressing **iNYK (Q1)** and **5YHP (W3)**, on a **uniformly random subset selected without reference to baseline outcomes**, run as **fresh end-to-end conversations** on a **model not used in the paper**, over **3 replicate runs** (raw accuracy): full context 87.5 +/- 2.0, **AC3-Reset 93.3 +/- 4.2**, **AC3-Gated-Reset 95.0 +/- 0.0**, with both operators ahead in every run.

* **Replication of the database result.** Addressing **iNYK (W1)**, the contested "exceeds the oracle" finding now replicates across all three models at 147 conversations per cell (AC3-Reset 49.0 / 56.2 / 55.1 vs. assistant omission 45.6 / 27.9 / 30.6), and holds at +26.0pp on the leak-free subset.

* **A WildChat judge audit.** Addressing **5YHP (W4)** and **Vg97 (Q2)**, 1,824 judgements across three judges from three families in both presentation orders, with positive controls: cross-family agreement 85.9–88.8% raw (PABAK 0.79–0.83, Gwet's AC1 0.84–0.87), self-consistency 96.9%, and an 82.5% win-rate under a punitive 2-of-3-in-both-orders rule.

* **Corrected tau2 reporting, and a withdrawal.** Addressing **iNYK (W3)** and **Vg97 (W2)**, we replaced best-of-3 with mean +/- std and re-ran the entire published tau2 matrix at N=3 (3 models x 5 arms x 3 replicate runs x 19 tasks, 855 scored rollouts). **Two of our three published baselines do not replicate** — DeepSeek-V4-Flash 31.6 → **70.2 ± 11.0**, Kimi-K2.6 26.3 → **78.9 ± 0.0** — and on all three models the re-measured baseline is at or above every AC3 arm, so **we withdraw the tau2 improvement claim** (correction 8 below). The noise floor the reviewers were pointing at is real and now quantified: at n=19 tasks a single cell has a binomial standard deviation of about 10.7pp. The structural result is unchanged, reproduces exactly, and does not depend on the baseline's level — **assistant omission scores 0% on every model**, in all nine cells and 171 rollouts, because omission destroys tool-call results that exist only in assistant turns, so those rollouts exhaust the step budget instead of terminating. We correct the abstract and introduction to the precise claim that AC3 is **the only method tested that improves over full context on every self-contained and referential benchmark, and the only method that remains viable in the stateful agentic setting**.

* **Presentation.** Addressing **Vg97** and **5YHP**, we move the structural-exclusion ablation into the main body, present memory explicitly as an optional ablated extension, and tighten the narrative so the stated scope and headline claims align.

## Corrections We Are Making to Our Own Reported Numbers

We think it is worth listing these together rather than leaving them scattered, because all of them come from experiments the reviews prompted.

1. **CollabLLM MATH-Hard.** At N=3, AC3-Augment **ties** full context (91.7 vs 91.7, identical 55/60 per-problem totals) rather than reaching the 100 we quoted from a single run on a near-ceiling benchmark. The regression 5YHP identified is refuted; an improvement is not claimed. On BigCodeBench the same re-run came out stronger (**+15pp in 3 of 3 replicates**, reproducing on a fully disjoint problem draw). We have additionally replicated the **assistant-omission** column at N=3 — the last single-run column in any of our tables — which narrows AC3's BigCodeBench margin over assistant omission to **+3.3pp**, inside the noise; we therefore claim no ordering between those two arms, only that both improve on full context.
2. **False-negative-adjusted accuracy.** The metric excludes items a judge deems under-specified from the *visible* messages only, so context-editing arms have far more of their failures excluded (62% vs 9% on one cell). We report **raw accuracy** throughout; an arm-symmetric re-judge reproduces the ordering with smaller magnitudes.
3. **The selective-editing claim does not hold for any operator, and we retract it.** The paper says AC3 "preserves what is correct and removes what is harmful." On constructed pollution AC3-Reset's edit precision is at chance with a 4.0% preservation rate, and AC3-Rewrite looked like the selective exception (27.0% removal, 38.9% preservation) — so our first fix was to re-attribute the claim to Rewrite. The counterfactual ablation on **naturally occurring** spans overturns that: Reset keeps 5 of 66 probe-admissible spans, **Rewrite keeps 0 of 66**, preservation on causally useful spans is 0% for both, and edit precision is exactly the base rate for both. Rewrite's apparent selectivity was an artifact of the injected spans' form — a short self-contained sentence survives compaction verbatim; the model's own prose and code get paraphrased. **AC3's mechanism, for both operators, is detect, discard the assistant side, and rebuild the specification from the user side**, and we will state it that way rather than retain the sentence on a technicality.
4. **WildChat.** Order-balanced re-judging moves the headline from 89.8 / 92.1 to **87.8 +/- 2.1 / 91.2 +/- 2.1**.
5. **Memory.** The reported +10 / +12pp effects are single-trial estimates below the learner's own ~6pp noise floor; we will re-run at N ≥ 4 or soften the claim.
6. **BigCodeBench scoring.** We previously stated that executable tests were unavailable on the CollabLLM harness. They are not: that path scores by real test execution, and we now report execution-based pass rates together with the scoring environment, because those pass rates are library-version sensitive.
7. **ERGO's denominators in the main LiC table.** ERGO — and only ERGO — was scored on unfiltered conversation pools, so it was charged with items no other method was asked to attempt. This one understates a baseline rather than flattering us. On the corrected pools **ERGO/math is 80.0 rather than 69.6**, which places it above AC3-Reset (75.0) and level with AC3-Gated-Reset (80.0); code is essentially unchanged (≈44.0) and database is untouched (12.0). The actions cell cannot be corrected — no filter artifact for that task has ever existed — and will be printed as an interval. We will print n per cell and note the original scoring in the caption. We also report the paired test alongside it: **no ERGO-vs-AC3 difference in that table is statistically distinguishable at n ≈ 20, in either direction** (code p = 0.375, math p = 1.00), which is true of the published table as well as the corrected one, and which we think is a more defensible thing to print than any ordering.
8. **The tau2-bench improvement claim is withdrawn, because two of our three published tau2 baselines do not replicate.** This is the largest correction in the list. Re-running the full published matrix at N=3 (3 models x 5 arms x 3 replicate runs x 19 tasks, 855 scored rollouts) moves the full-context baselines from 31.6 to **70.2 ± 11.0** (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and from 26.3 to **78.9 ± 0.0** (Kimi-K2.6), and **on all three models the re-measured baseline is at or above every AC3 arm**. We had compared a clean treatment against a control our own logs record as rate-limit-clipped (14/20 and 19/20 short-exits). That this is a statement about our numbers rather than about comparability was tested, not assumed: gpt-5.4's baseline reproduces at 68.4 against a published 68.4, assistant omission reproduces at 0.0 across all nine cells, no model was substituted, and the invocation strings are byte-identical to our committed sweep scripts. **On tau2 we therefore claim no improvement.** What survives, untouched because it does not depend on the baseline's level, is the structural result: **assistant omission is 0% on every model** and fails by exhausting the step budget. Two disclosures travel with this — the gpt-5.4 Gated-Reset regression iNYK identified **reproduces in direction** (57.9 ± 21.1 vs 68.4, paired −10.5pp, p = 0.238) and we keep it; and on gpt-5.4 the baseline reproduced exactly while every AC3 arm fell 10 to 37 points, which **we could not explain** after ruling out model substitution, non-firing, degenerate termination and rate limits, and after finding and patching a real fork defect worth only +2.3pp. We report it as unexplained.

We are grateful to the reviewers for feedback that made these improvements concrete, and we hope the additions address the concerns raised. We are happy to run further analyses during the discussion period.
