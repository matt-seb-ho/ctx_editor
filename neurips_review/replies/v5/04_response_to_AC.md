# Response to the Area Chair

We thank the Area Chair for coordinating the review process and for the candid meta-review. We are grateful for the explicit invitation to rebut, and we have used the discussion period to address each of the three reservations with new experiments rather than with argument alone. We summarise our response here and point to where the evidence appears.

## On the validity of theoretical assumptions

We want to make sure we address this reservation correctly, and we would welcome clarification if we have misread it, since no reviewer raised an objection framed in formal or theoretical terms.

Our best reading is that this refers to Reviewer 5YHP's W1, which concerns the **scope condition for structural exclusion**: that a task specification can be recovered from user turns alone when those turns independently specify the task. If so, we would make two observations.

First, this is an **empirical and falsifiable condition rather than a formal assumption**, and the paper tests it directly and adversarially rather than assuming it. Table 5 shows that prompt-level instructions to ignore assistant turns are insufficient and that the structural constraint is what carries the effect, a prediction that could have failed and did not. Appendix D reports what happens when the condition does not hold, using the soft-attention variant that must see the full conversation. Testing a method past the boundary of its own stated scope is, we would suggest, the opposite of leaving an assumption unexamined.

Since the meta-review we have also tested the mechanism assumption that sits underneath it — whether the analyzer is auditing the conversation or simply re-solving the task — and reported the result including where it goes against us. Restricted to conversations where the analyzer verifiably neither states the correct answer nor reveals the gold value, AC3 gains **+30.2pp on code (0% leakage across 106 conversations)** and **+26.0pp on database**, pooling to **+20.7pp [+14.8, +25.3], p < 0.0001** on 329 conversations. On **math** the same test comes out at **−2.6pp**, and we concede it explicitly: on grade-school arithmetic, auditing and solving are not separable. We would rather present a mechanism claim that holds on two of three tasks and say so, than a blanket one.

Second, we would note that **context pollution is a phenomenon characterised empirically rather than formally**, and that our contribution is comparable in kind to the work it builds on, for example Laban et al. (2025) on multi-turn degradation and Huang et al. (2026) on assistant omission. Our contribution is in the same register: falsifiable predictions tested against controlled experiments. We do not claim a formal result, and we would be glad to state the scope condition more explicitly in Section 3 so that its empirical status is unambiguous.

If the Area Chair had a different concern in mind, we would very much appreciate the opportunity to address it specifically during the discussion period.

## On generalizability

The concern that the method changes for each setting was the reservation listed first, and we agree it deserved a direct empirical answer rather than a description. We now provide two.

The same two-query analyzer and the same four operators are evaluated across **3 respondent models x 4 LiC tasks, plus CollabLLM, WildChat, and tau2-bench**, using a single code path with no per-benchmark tuning. What varies is operator intensity, which is a documented knob.

We also tested generalizability at the component level, which we think is the sharper version of the question. Holding the respondent model fixed and swapping only the analyzer across **five models from four families**, every analyzer produces a positive and individually significant gain over full context, from **+12.9pp to +39.9pp** (n=178 matched pairs, exact McNemar, all p < 0.001), and none falls below baseline. Weak analyzers **under-detect rather than mis-detect**, which is why the degradation is graceful. The best analyzer is Kimi-K2.6 and the weakest is an OpenAI model; since the respondent is also non-OpenAI, the best configuration contains no OpenAI model anywhere. Details in **Common Weakness 1** and our reply to **Vg97 (Q3)**.

The two configurations reviewers flagged, CollabLLM and tau2, are the stated theory being applied rather than departures from it, and a component-level table separating the essential parts from the single tunable knob is in **Common Weakness 1**.

Because several of the corrections below narrow individual comparisons against assistant omission, we have also assembled the whole AC3-vs-assistant-omission picture in one place, in **Common Weakness 2**, rather than leave it distributed across six documents. In summary: across the full 36-triple matrix the head-to-head is close to a wash (+2.6pp, 15 wins / 17 losses / 4 ties), and we say so ourselves — at the level of individual problems it is **+2.8pp with a 95% CI of [−0.3, +5.9]**, an interval that includes zero, which is why we do not claim it; the separation is concentrated exactly where our analysis predicts — **+18.7pp on LiC-database (8 of 9 triples), whose problem-clustered interval is [+10.7, +26.6]** —, assistant omission at **0% and structurally broken on tau2**, and every populated cell of our per-respondent WildChat table favouring AC3. The claim we defend is not that AC3 beats assistant omission everywhere, but that **assistant omission is the right operator in exactly one regime and our framework identifies that regime in advance**, from a property of the setting observable before any run. A method that predicts a strong baseline's successes as well as its structural failures is a different contribution from one that must out-score it in every cell.

**One thing about the condensation experiment we should flag ourselves, because it is visible from our own tables.** Unlike Table 1, it runs on the **complete LiC pool with no instance selection**, which is why its full-context baseline (56.1% database / 83.0% code) is far above Table 1's (4.0% / 15.8%) and above the 19.0–22.4% in our reply to Reviewer iNYK. All three are correct measurements of deliberately different populations, and the dominant term is pool difficulty selection rather than model era, evaluator version, or metric — which we verified by restriction rather than argument:

| Where it appears | Full-context accuracy | Population |
|---|---|---|
| Table 1 (submission) | 4.0 / 15.8 | 25 instances per task, selected for **highest** full-context failure rate under GPT-5-mini; last-turn replay |
| Reply to iNYK (3-model matrix) | 19.0–22.4 | 50 instances per task selected the same way from GPT-5.2 logs, replay prefixes further weighted toward baseline failures |
| Condensation experiment (**Common Weakness 5**) | 56.1 / 83.0 | The **complete, unselected** pool (107 / 100), full end-to-end simulation |

Restricting the third run to Table 1's exact 25 database instances — same model, same evaluator, same conversations — moves it from 56.1% to **32.0%** (code 83.0% → 48.0%), and LiC's own released logs independently put GPT-5-mini at 29.9% on the full pool against 4.0% on its top-25 subset: two routes to a ≈25pp selection effect, agreeing within 2pp. **Absolute accuracies are not comparable across these venues; the paired Δ within each block is.** And the unselected pool is not an easy setting — its measured single-turn ceiling is 94.4% / 98.0%, so it still carries a **38.3pp / 15.0pp** multi-turn gap, and AC3-Reset closes **51% / 60%** of that gap against **50%** on Table 1's much harder subset. **Our baselines move 52 points across venues; the fraction of the gap we close moves by ten points at most.** If anything the unselected pool makes the comparison harder for us, since most of its instances are not pollution-limited at all, which dilutes any Δ — and AC3-Reset still beats full context by +19.6pp and both condensation budgets by +22.4 to +28.0pp on the same conversations.

We also want to put six corrections in front of the Area Chair directly, because we think how a submission handles its own disconfirming measurements is relevant to the tractability question. Five of them move against our own numbers — including one, the last, that withdraws a headline result on an entire benchmark — and the remaining one (item 5) moves against us by raising a baseline we compare against.

1. **A CollabLLM figure we quoted did not replicate.** At N=3, AC3-Augment on MATH-Hard **ties** full context (91.7 vs 91.7; identical 55/60 per-problem totals) rather than reaching the 100 we reported from a single run on a near-ceiling benchmark. It still answers the regression Reviewer 5YHP identified — no arm degrades — but we withdraw the improvement claim. On BigCodeBench the same re-run came out *stronger*: **+15pp in 3 of 3 replicates**, reproducing on a fully disjoint problem draw. We have since also replicated the **assistant-omission** column of that table at N=3 (it was our last single-run column anywhere), which narrows AC3's margin over assistant omission on BigCodeBench from +6.7pp to **+3.3pp** — inside the noise — so we no longer claim an ordering between those two arms and rest the result on the comparison against full context.
2. **Our false-negative-adjusted accuracy metric is biased in our own favour** and we are removing it from headline reporting. It excludes items a judge deems under-specified using only the *visible* messages, so arms that hide content have more of their failures excluded — 62% for AC3-Reset against 9% for baseline on one cell. An arm-symmetric re-judge reproduces the raw ordering but shrinks magnitudes (one cell 89.0% → 77.1%). Every number in this rebuttal is raw. **We should be precise about the blast radius, because it is narrower than the concession sounds and we would rather state the boundary than have it assumed away.** Table 1's denominators (20/19/25/23) are **not** produced by this per-run metric. They come from a **pool-level pre-filter** computed once on baseline traces and applied identically to every arm before any method runs — an arm-symmetric filter that reproduces those denominators exactly, and one we stand behind and will document. The per-run adjustment touches at most four cells of Table 1 by at most one sample each, and two of those four move in favour of prior work. What we withdraw is the per-run metric as a *reported statistic*; the main table is unaffected.
3. **One sentence in the paper is wrong, and our first attempt to fix it was also wrong.** The paper says AC3 "preserves what is correct and removes what is harmful." Our constructed-pollution study showed that is not true of AC3-**Reset** (edit precision at chance, preservation 4.0%), and on that evidence we intended to re-attribute the claim to AC3-**Rewrite**, which looked selective on those spans. A second, harder study has now measured the same thing on **naturally occurring** spans, causally, with no detector and no judge anywhere in the label path — 111 spans in 30 conversations, each re-run 14× present and 12× removed — and **neither operator is selective**: Reset keeps 5 of 66 probe-admissible spans, Rewrite keeps **0 of 66**, preservation on causally useful spans is 0% for both, and edit precision is exactly the base rate for both. **We therefore retract the re-attribution as well as the original claim.** Rewrite looked selective on the injected spans because a short self-contained sentence can be copied across verbatim by a compacting operator; on the model's own prose and code it paraphrases and nothing distinctive survives. AC3's actual mechanism, for both operators, is *detect, discard the assistant side, rebuild the specification from the user side* — and we will describe it that way rather than keep the sentence on a technicality. The same study is also the strongest mechanism evidence we have: it establishes causally that natural pollution is **real and concentrated** (per-span effect spread 0.155 against a matched null's 0.125, p = 0.0085; 16 large-effect spans where the null predicts 9.3, p = 0.017) and that AC3 removes **100%** of the spans the ablation proves harmful. Reviewer 5YHP asked for exactly this measurement; it answered them, including the part that cost us.
4. **WildChat's headline moves down slightly** under a full order-balanced re-judge, from 89.8 / 92.1 to 87.8 / 91.2.
5. **A scoring error in our main LiC table understated a baseline we compare against.** ERGO — and only ERGO — was evaluated on unfiltered conversation pools, while every other row used pools with a small number of items pre-filtered by a procedure that is computed once on baseline traces and applied identically to every arm. We measured the correction rather than estimating it — placing ERGO on the filtered pool has exactly one unknown, how many of the excluded items ERGO was solving, and we replayed ERGO against those items directly: **ERGO/math moves from 69.6 to 80.0, which places it above AC3-Reset (75.0) and level with AC3-Gated-Reset (80.0)**. Code is essentially unchanged (≈44.0), database is untouched (12.0), and the actions cell cannot be corrected at all and will be printed as an interval. We also ran the paired test, and it is the part we would most like the Area Chair to see: **no ERGO-vs-AC3 difference in that table is statistically distinguishable at n ≈ 20, in either direction** (code p = 0.375, math p = 1.00) — true of the published table as well as the corrected one. We will print n per cell, disclose the original scoring in the caption, and stop presenting that ordering as settled. This one is recoverable from our own printed percentages by anyone with a calculator, which is precisely why we would rather state it than be shown it.
6. **We withdraw the tau2 improvement claim outright, because two of our three published tau2 baselines do not replicate.** This is the largest of the six and we would rather the Area Chair have it from us. We re-ran the entire published tau2 matrix at N=3 — 3 models x 5 arms x 3 replicate runs x 19 tasks, **855 scored rollouts**, all 15 cells complete — and the full-context baselines move from 31.6 to **70.2 ± 11.0** (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and from 26.3 to **78.9 ± 0.0** (Kimi-K2.6). **On all three models the re-measured baseline is at or above every AC3 arm**; AC3-Augment is significantly *worse* than baseline on all three. We had compared a clean treatment against a degraded control: our own logs record 14/20 and 19/20 short-exits on those two published baseline cells, and our internal report already described them as floors rather than honest performance. We are not softening this into "mixed results" — **on tau2 we cannot claim an improvement, and the magnitude comparison is withdrawn from the paper.** The distinction between "our numbers were wrong" and "these runs are not comparable" was tested rather than assumed: gpt-5.4's baseline reproduces at **68.4** against a published 68.4, assistant omission reproduces at **0.0 across all nine cells / 171 rollouts**, the model identities were reachable and unsubstituted, and the invocation strings are byte-identical to our committed sweep scripts. What survives is the result we most need tau2 for, and it is untouched because it does not depend on the baseline's level: **blanket assistant omission scores 0% on every model and fails structurally**, its rollouts exhausting the step budget because omission destroys tool-call results that exist only in assistant turns. Two further disclosures travel with this. The **gpt-5.4 Gated-Reset regression Reviewer iNYK identified reproduces in direction** (57.9 ± 21.1 vs 68.4, paired −10.5pp, p = 0.238) and we keep it rather than dropping it. And on gpt-5.4 the *baseline* reproduced exactly while every AC3 arm fell 10 to 37 points, which **we could not explain**: we ruled out model substitution, the operator not firing, degenerate termination and rate limits, and the one real defect we found in our tau2 fork (53% of analyzer calls falling back to splicing a raw completion into the briefing) is worth only +2.3pp when patched. We report it as unexplained. Consequently we also correct the sharpened claim in the abstract: AC3 is the only method tested that improves over full context on every self-contained and referential benchmark, and the only method that remains **viable** in the stateful agentic setting — not one that improves across the entire spectrum.
